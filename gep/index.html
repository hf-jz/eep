Certified equilibrium propagation

When is an equilibrium-propagation update trustworthy?

Non-conservative dynamics, finite nudging, several stable equilibria. The paper derives the exact gradient conditions, budgets the finite-perturbation error, and returns either a certified interval or a refusal.

What is proved

Exact conditions, a computable budget, a branch-aware certificate

Exact gradient and the sensitivity-adjoint gap Theorems 2, 4, 5

For a smooth non-conservative system with a regular equilibrium, the gradient is d* = B transpose A inverse transpose q. The centred contrast of the uncorrected nudged system returns the sensitivity A inverse q instead, and the difference B transpose (A inverse - A inverse transpose) q is exactly the bias of vector-field EP. Conservative systems hide the gap because A = A transpose.

Two quantities that differ by a transpose. On the benchmark the uncorrected rule is wrong by 12 percent relative while its residual is 1e-17.

Finite nudge, computable constant Theorem 9, Proposition 10

The corrected learning phase has Jacobian A transpose at the free equilibrium, so its centred contrast carries the adjoint direction, and the remainder is second order: the error is bounded by (B norm / 6) x3 beta squared, with x3 obtained by implicit differentiation and interval evaluation rather than fitted.

Observed order 2.002 on the three-state benchmark, and 2.0006 in the adaptive controller, against the predicted 2.

Exact stationarity at finite nudge Theorems 7, 8

In the doubled state space, stationarity forces the midpoint onto the free equilibrium and gives A transpose d = beta grad C. Hence d/beta equals the adjoint exactly, at any nonzero beta. The residual form of the same statement, d/beta minus the adjoint bounded by (A inverse transpose norm) times the residual over beta, carries the exact constant 55/51.

The 1/beta factor is why smaller nudging is not automatically better, and why the budget has an interior optimum at beta* = (d / 2a) to the power one third.

Certificates and non-normality Theorems 12, 13, 14, 15

A Krawczyk inclusion on a box proves existence and uniqueness of the free equilibrium, of both perturbation endpoints, and uniformly over a whole parameter interval. The algorithm is fail-closed. Separately, a family with fixed eigenvalues -1 and -0.1 amplifies the adjoint response by 15.5, so finite-time bounds must come from the semigroup or an integrator certificate, not from the spectrum.

The Lyapunov envelope stays valid, and stayed four orders of magnitude loose.

Error budget

Every term is certified, none is fitted

The total bound is the sum of a contrast term, a root-enclosure term, a finite-time term and a rounding term. On the three-state benchmark the certified run accepts beta = 0.005 with T = 6 and a total bound of 4.391e-4 against a tolerance of 5e-4, while the observed gradient error is 1.076e-5: the certificate is 41 times more conservative than reality and covers it. A shorter horizon was tried first and rejected, because its solve term alone exceeded the tolerance.

Benchmarks

Two systems, both with exact or independently computed answers

Multistable rational systemF = (x - x cubed + y/5 + theta, -y + (x - x cubed)/10)

Equilibria exactly (-1,0), (0,0) and (1,0); the outer two stable, the middle a saddle. Exact negative gradients 67/102 and -11/34, computed in rational arithmetic rather than estimated. Both branch tubes certified over beta in [-0.02, 0.02].

Three-state non-normal systemF = A0 (z - z*) + 0.25 tanh(K (z - z*))

Eigenvalues -1.8097 plus minus 0.9749i and -1.6805, with a strongly non-normal Jacobian. The implicit gradient agrees with automatic differentiation to 1.8e-16, the uncorrected vector-field rule has relative error 1.55, and the exact rational certificate bounds the gradient error at 4.391e-4.

Integrators and noiseadaptive RK4, fixed RK4, DOP853, 500 noise trials

The adaptive controller reaches 7.9e-7 gradient accuracy with 168 evaluations against 1136 for a fixed step and 8500 for the reference, and a bounded state-noise stress test satisfies its triangle bound in all 500 trials. These are floating-point diagnostics, not certificates, and the page does not present them as such.

Scope

What the paper claims, and what it does not

Claimed

  • Exact gradient conditions for a broad class of smooth non-conservative equilibrium systems with regular isolated equilibria.
  • A finite-perturbation error budget whose constants are computed by interval evaluation.
  • Branch existence, uniqueness, endpoint attribution and stability certified over a parameter interval.
  • A fail-closed acceptance rule: a certified interval with an error bound, or a refusal.
  • Two benchmarks reproduced by one command, including the rejection paths.

Not claimed

  • No new equilibrium-propagation update rule. The antisymmetry-corrected phase and the doubled-state construction are prior work, cited and used as objects of certification.
  • No global convergence statement for arbitrary nonlinear dynamics.
  • No optimality claim for the selected nudging strength.
  • No transfer of the numerical certificates to systems whose coefficients are estimated from data.
  • No performance comparison with backpropagation, and no neural-network accuracy claim.
